# Industry proof: big brands using AI for content (19 Nov 2026)

Use these to back up "a content team's output, without the payroll". Quote only the company's own figure and name the source.

## The article Kunal shared
Storyboard18, 18 Nov 2026: Unilever creates 12 times more content than in 2024 at 90% lower cost. The company's own source: https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2026/how-unilever-is-creating-demand-systematically-at-scale/

- **Speed:** a video that used to take 3 weeks now takes 3 hours.
- **The "AI content engine":** brand identity, propositions and messaging are coded into the engine, so every audience, platform and market gets its own content.
- **Creators:** 500,000 creators, up from 75,000 in 2024. Creator content returns about 1.5 times what brand-made content does.
- **Dove × Reddit:** Dove promised to publish the first 50 reviews unedited. The product went from #3 to #1 hair mask in the US.
- **Brands showing up in AI answers:** Unilever structured 150,000 science documents so AI assistants recommend its brands.
- **The human line:** "Technology can make content, optimise spend and recommend products. But it can't make a brand matter to people." (CMO Leandro Barreto.) This matches our No-Slop stance.

## Similar stories
- **Mondelez (Oreo, Milka, Chips Ahoy):** about $40M on an in-house generative AI platform, with Accenture and Publicis. It cuts content costs by an expected 30–50%. Humans review everything, and no human likenesses are generated. https://mexicobusiness.news/agribusiness/news/mondelez-uses-generative-ai-cut-marketing-costs-50

## Use with care
- Most of the cost figures in vendor blogs (80–95% savings) are unverified. Only Unilever's and Mondelez's come from the companies themselves or from news reports.
- The UAE Media Council reportedly restricts AI depictions of national symbols, leaders and public figures without approval. Check the Council's own rules before any National Day content.
